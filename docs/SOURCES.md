# Sources and fact notes

Every on-screen number was checked against at least one source below. Where sources disagree, the film uses the range or the more conservative figure.

## Sun (chapters 1–7)
| Fact shown | Source |
|---|---|
| Corona > 1,000,000 °C; photosphere 4,500–6,000 °C; chromosphere 1,000–2,000 km thick, spicules up to 10,000 km; tachocline separates solid-body rotation from latitude-dependent rotation | ESA, *Anatomy of our Sun* — esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2019/10/Anatomy_of_our_Sun |
| Convection zone: outer ~30 % of the radius, ~200,000 km deep, 2,000,000 °C at the base; radiative zone 25 %–70 % of the radius; tachocline is where the magnetic field is thought to be generated | NASA MSFC — solarscience.msfc.nasa.gov/interior.shtml |
| Core ≈ 15 million °C; 3 % of volume, 34 % of mass, 99 % of fusion power; pp-chain releases 26.7 MeV; CNO < 10 % of the Sun's energy; ~4 million tonnes of mass converted per second | Wikipedia *Solar core*; scienceblog.com photon-journey article; ESA |
| Photon escape time: 10,000–170,000 years (ESA: ~170,000; arXiv 2012.09568: 20,000–200,000). Neutrinos leave in ~2 s | ESA; arxiv.org/abs/2012.09568; scienceblog.com |
| pp-chain ~ T⁴, CNO ~ T¹⁶; CNO dominates above ~1.3 M☉ / ~17 million K | astronomy course notes (Varsity Tutors) summarising standard stellar-structure results |
| Helioseismology (sound waves map the interior) | NYU/IDM "Structure of the Sun" summary; standard result |

## Where sources disagree / what is *not* sourced to a fetched page
- **Silicon burning duration:** Table 13-1 says 1 day (25 M☉); Wikipedia says ~5 days for 25 M☉; a 15 M☉ model gives 4.5 days. The film says "about one day" and labels it the *25-sun model*.
- **Core pressure ≈ 250 billion atmospheres**, **Sun's diameter ≈ 1.4 million km**, **109 Earths across / ~1.3 million by volume**, **red dwarf lifetime "trillions of years"**, **Sun ≈ 10 billion years**: standard textbook values quoted from general knowledge, *not* from a page fetched during this build. Spot-check before publishing if you want them cited.
- Temperature/density readouts in the HUD between the anchor points (e.g. "≈ 3.9 million °C" in the radiative zone) are interpolated from a simplified table (core 15 M°C, 7 M°C at the base of the radiative zone, 2 M°C at the tachocline, 5,500 °C at the surface). They are illustrative.
- The collapse is **slowed down** on screen (the film says so on screen); the real collapse takes ~0.2 s.
- The corona-heating question is genuinely unsolved; the narration says "scientists are still debating it".
